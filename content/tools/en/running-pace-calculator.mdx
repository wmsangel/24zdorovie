## Pace, speed and predictions — what the calculator gives

From a single run it answers three questions.

**Pace** — minutes per kilometre (min/km). This is the runner's working unit: training and race splits are planned in pace. A lower number is faster.

**Speed** — kilometres per hour (km/h). The same thing, but the familiar form for a gym treadmill, which is set in km/h.

**Predicted times** for 5K, 10K, half and full marathon — an estimate of what to expect at other distances on your current form.

## How the prediction works: Riegel's formula

The prediction uses Peter Riegel's formula (1981):

<DataTable
  head={["Term", "Meaning"]}
  rows={[
    ["T₂ = T₁ × (D₂ / D₁)^1.06", "Time at the new distance"],
    ["T₁, D₁", "Your time and distance from the run"],
    ["D₂", "The distance you're predicting for"],
    ["1.06", "Exponent: pace slows slightly as distance grows"],
  ]}
  caption="Riegel's formula works well from roughly 1500 m to the marathon"
/>

The 1.06 exponent captures a simple fact: the longer the distance, the slightly slower your average pace — you can't hold 5K pace over a marathon. So a marathon predicted from a 5K is always slower than simple multiplication.

## Where the prediction is wrong

The formula assumes you're equally prepared for every distance. In practice you're not:

<KeyPoints title="What the formula ignores" items={[
  "Mileage: without long runs, a marathon will be slower than predicted",
  "Fuelling and hydration — decisive over the marathon",
  "Sprints and ultras: beyond 1500 m … 42 km the formula drifts",
  "Heat, wind, terrain and altitude"
]} />

The practical takeaway: a prediction to a neighbouring distance (10K to half marathon) is usually close, while a jump 'from 5K to marathon' should be read as a ceiling, not a plan.

## Using pace in training

The same running speed does different jobs depending on your heart rate. Most of your mileage is best run slowly — in 'zone 2', where you can hold a conversation. It's easy running that builds endurance, not constant all-out effort.
