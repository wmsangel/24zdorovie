## What the range depends on

The key factor is your **pre-pregnancy BMI**. The higher it is, the lower the recommended gain: a body with fat reserves doesn't need to add as much as one that's underweight. So there's no single "number of kilos" — there's a range for your starting weight.

## If you're outside the range

A one-off swing isn't a problem. But a consistent move outside the range is worth raising with your doctor: excessive gain is linked to gestational diabetes, a large baby and high blood pressure, while too little is linked to low birth weight. The goal is a healthy trend under supervision, not an exact number on the scale.

<Callout type="warning" title="A guide, not a target to stress over">
  The calculator gives the IOM range for a singleton pregnancy. Twins are higher, and your own circumstances are followed by your doctor. Don't force your weight to a number — watch the trend and how you feel.
</Callout>
