## How the next period date is worked out

It comes down to cycle length: **the first day of your last period + your cycle length**. A cycle is counted from the first day of one period to the first day of the next, not from when bleeding ends. The average is 28 days, but the normal range is wide: 21 to 35.

The calculator projects that length forward and shows not just the next date but several cycles ahead, plus your current cycle day — which helps you see where you are: menstruation, the follicular phase, ovulation or the luteal phase.

Regularity matters more than the exact number. A consistently short (< 21 days) or long (> 35 days) cycle, or big month-to-month swings, is worth raising with a gynaecologist — PCOS, thyroid problems and other causes can be behind it.

## Why the prediction is a guide, not an exact date

Ovulation — which sets when the next period starts — shifts with stress, illness, travel, hard training and weight change. So even on a regular cycle the real date wanders by a few days. On an irregular cycle the calendar is only a rough frame.

<Callout type="warning" title="A calendar is not contraception">
  Predicting periods is no good for preventing pregnancy: ovulation shifts and sperm stay viable for up to 5 days, so there are almost no reliably 'safe' days. Use proper contraception for that.
</Callout>
