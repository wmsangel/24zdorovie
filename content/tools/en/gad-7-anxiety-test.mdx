## How the score works

GAD-7 asks seven questions about how often, over the last two weeks, you've been bothered by typical signs of anxiety. Each answer runs from "not at all" (0) to "nearly every day" (3). The 0–21 total maps to a severity level.

## What to do with your result

A low score is a cue to keep what works and retake the test if you feel worse. From 10 points up, it's worth discussing how you feel with a doctor or therapist. Anxiety responds well to treatment, and the earlier you start, the easier it is.

<Callout type="info" title="Anxiety and depression often go together">
  Anxiety disorders frequently come with low mood. If you also feel down, take a depression screen as well — and show both results to a clinician.
</Callout>

## What actually helps

Mild anxiety responds to sleep routine, movement, and breathing and relaxation practices. Moderate and severe forms respond to evidence-based psychotherapy (CBT above all) and, at a clinician's discretion, medication.

<Callout type="warning" title="This is a screen, not a diagnosis">
  GAD-7 measures symptom severity; it doesn't diagnose or tell types of anxiety disorder apart. A clinician makes the diagnosis. Your score is a reason to discuss how you feel with a professional, not to diagnose or rule it out yourself.
</Callout>
