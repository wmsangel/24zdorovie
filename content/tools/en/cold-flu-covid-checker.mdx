## What tells them apart, and what does not

A cold is most reliably given away by its **onset and symptom mix**. It builds gradually, starting in the nose and throat, with little fever. The flu is the opposite: an abrupt start, high fever, aches and exhaustion, with a runny nose in the background.

The flu and COVID-19, though, **cannot be separated by symptoms**. Both cause fever, cough, weakness, headache and aches. The one semi-specific difference is loss of smell in COVID, and even that is unreliable. So the tool honestly flags when your checked symptoms fit all three equally.

<Callout type="warning" title="This is not a diagnosis">
  The tool matches your symptoms to typical patterns — nothing more. It does not diagnose and does not replace a test or an exam. With pronounced flu or COVID symptoms, a test, not a set of checkboxes, decides what to do.

## When not to guess, but to get help

Some signs matter more than any "symptom diagnosis". Trouble breathing, chest pain, confusion and bluish lips are reasons to seek care immediately, whether it is a cold, the flu or COVID.

## What helps and what does not

Most "immune boosters" and megadoses of vitamin C do not prevent colds or shorten them to any meaningful degree. What actually works is sleep, movement, diet, and vaccination against flu and COVID for those for whom it is indicated.

## Who should take care

The tool is designed for adults without serious chronic illness. Children, pregnant people and those with lung or heart disease should see a doctor sooner for respiratory symptoms rather than rely on self-assessment: for them an ordinary cold more often leads to complications.
