## What "ideal weight" is and why it's a range

The query "ideal weight by height" assumes there is one correct number. There
isn't. At the same height, a body can weigh differently depending on the share of
muscle, bone density and build — and all of it can be healthy. That is why
evidence-based medicine talks not about an "ideal" but about a **healthy weight
range**.

The simplest and most widely accepted reference is body mass index (BMI). A BMI
range of 18.5 to 24.9 is considered healthy for most adults. Multiply these bounds
by the square of your height in metres and you get the healthy weight range for
your specific height. The calculator above does this automatically.

## The classic formulas: where "one number" came from

The Devine, Robinson, Miller and Hamwi formulas that still surface in online
calculators were created in the 1960s–80s and **not to tell a person how much they
should weigh**. Their main purpose was clinical — to calculate a drug dose from
lean body weight so that medication was dosed by lean mass rather than fat.
