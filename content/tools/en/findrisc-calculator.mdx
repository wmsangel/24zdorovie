  - q: "How many points is a lot?"

  - q: "Do I need a blood test to take it?"
    a: "No. FINDRISC uses what you already know about yourself: age, height and weight (for BMI), waist circumference, and habits. A blood test comes at the next step — to confirm or rule out prediabetes/diabetes if the questionnaire flags an elevated risk."

    a: "Yes, substantially.   - q: "Who is the test not for?"

## How the score works

FINDRISC adds up points across eight items. The heaviest weights go to the factors most tied to diabetes: a past episode of high blood glucose (5 points) and diabetes in close relatives (up to 5 points). Age, weight, waist and lifestyle make up the rest. The maximum is 26 points.

## What to do with your result

Low and slightly elevated risk is a cue to keep what works and retake the test in a year or two. From 12 points (moderate risk and up) it's worth getting a **fasting glucose** or **HbA1c** test and discussing it with a doctor: the prediabetes stage is exactly where lifestyle change pays off most.

## What actually lowers the risk

The good news is that half of the FINDRISC items are modifiable. In the Finnish Diabetes Prevention Study, lifestyle change roughly halved diabetes incidence among at-risk people. The levers are the same boring ones that fight visceral fat and heart risk: losing 5–7% of body weight, moving regularly, more fiber, and fewer sugary drinks.

<Callout type="warning" title="This is a screen, not a diagnosis">
  FINDRISC estimates probability; it does not measure blood sugar or diagnose anything. Only a blood test and a doctor can confirm or rule out diabetes. A high score is a prompt to get checked, not a verdict.
</Callout>
