## How the score works

PHQ-9 asks nine questions about how often, over the last two weeks, you've been bothered by typical signs of depression. Each answer runs from "not at all" (0) to "nearly every day" (3). The 0–27 total maps to a severity level.

## What to do with your result

A low score is a cue to keep what works and retake the test if you feel worse. From 10 points up, it's worth discussing how you feel with a doctor or therapist. The score isn't a verdict or a diagnosis — it's a way to see the scale and decide.

<Callout type="danger" title="If you have thoughts of harming yourself">
  This matters and there's no shame in it. Don't stay with it alone — get help now: someone you trust, a doctor, or emergency services. In the US call or text **988**; in the UK call **111** or Samaritans on **116 123**; elsewhere call your local emergency number.
</Callout>

## What actually helps

Depression is treatable. Mild forms respond to sleep routine, movement and support from people you trust; moderate and severe forms to evidence-based psychotherapy and, at a clinician's discretion, medication. One of the best-studied methods is cognitive behavioural therapy.

<Callout type="warning" title="This is a screen, not a diagnosis">
  PHQ-9 measures symptom severity; it doesn't diagnose or tell depression apart from other conditions with similar signs. A clinician makes the diagnosis. Your score is a reason to discuss how you feel with a professional, not to diagnose or rule it out yourself.
</Callout>
