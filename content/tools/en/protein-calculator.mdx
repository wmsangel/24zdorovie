## How the calculation works

A protein target is not a single number but a range of grams per kilogram of body weight. The calculator takes a base range from your activity level and shifts it for your goal.

- **Sedentary** — 0.8–1.0 g/kg. The lower bound is the RDA: the minimum at which the average healthy person avoids deficiency, not an optimum for wellbeing or body composition.
- **Regular training** — 1.2–1.6 g/kg. The general recommendation for physically active people.
- **Strength, building or keeping muscle** — 1.6–2.2 g/kg. The upper bound applies in a calorie deficit and during active gaining.

A calorie deficit adds a margin to preserve muscle; muscle gain sits at the top of the range. After 65 the lower bound never drops below 1.0 g/kg regardless of activity.

<Callout type="science" title="Why not 0.8 g/kg">
  The 0.8 g/kg RDA was derived by nitrogen balance as the amount at which 97.5% of healthy people avoid deficiency. That is a disease threshold, not a health target. Studies on body composition, age-related muscle preservation and recovery consistently favour higher intakes.
</Callout>

## How to spread protein across the day

Total daily intake matters more than distribution, but distribution helps: muscle protein synthesis is maximally stimulated by a serving of about 0.3–0.4 g/kg in one sitting. In practice that means 3–5 protein-containing meals rather than the whole day's protein at dinner.

The "only 30 g per meal" rule is a myth. The body absorbs essentially all protein; the point is only that above 0.3–0.4 g/kg the extra stimulation of muscle protein synthesis is small. What you eat is not lost — it serves other functions.

## Who this does not apply to

In chronic kidney disease protein is restricted and the target comes from a doctor — it can be considerably lower than the calculated range. Pregnancy, dialysis, phenylketonuria and several metabolic conditions are handled separately. In all of these, the calculator is not a guide.
