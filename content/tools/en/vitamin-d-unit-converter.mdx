## How the vitamin D unit conversion works

There is just one number to remember: **1 microgram (mcg) of vitamin D = 40 international units (IU)**. Everything else follows from it:

- to turn **mcg into IU**, multiply by 40;
- to turn **IU into mcg**, divide by 40;
- **milligrams** only come up because people search for them: 1 mg = 1000 mcg, so 1000 IU = 25 mcg = 0.025 mg.

The same factor is defined for both forms of the vitamin, D2 (ergocalciferol) and D3 (cholecalciferol) — they differ in how well they raise your blood level, not in the unit maths.

So a bottle labelled "25 mcg" and one labelled "1000 IU" contain the same dose — a frequent source of confusion when comparing products or following a foreign recommendation.

## Why milligrams almost never appear

Vitamin D is active in tiny amounts, so a milligram would be a huge dose: 1 mg is 40,000 IU, ten times the daily upper limit. That is why labels and guidelines use **mcg or IU**, and a search like "25 mcg in mg" (0.025 mg) usually means someone is trying to line up two labels, not dose in milligrams.

## A conversion, not a dose

This tool only changes the units. **It does not tell you how much vitamin D to take** — that depends on your 25(OH)D blood level, body weight, skin, latitude and the season. For a personalised target use the dose calculator, and confirm anything above everyday prevention with a doctor.
