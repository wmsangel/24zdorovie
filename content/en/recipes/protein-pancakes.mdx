Pancakes are rarely associated with a high-protein breakfast, but they are easy to turn into one — with no sports protein powder and no sugar. Cottage cheese, eggs and oats in a blender make a batter that yields a serving with 30 g of protein. The whole secret is to let the batter rest and not rush the flip.

## Protein without a tub of powder

The idea of a high-protein breakfast often stalls on sports protein, but it is not needed here. Cottage cheese provides most of the protein, eggs add both protein and binding, and oats bring complex carbs and fiber. Together that is 30 g of protein per serving from foods available in any shop.

<Callout type="tip" title="Let the batter rest">
  Five minutes of resting makes all the difference: the oats absorb moisture, the batter thickens and the pancakes hold their shape. Cook it straight away and the batter spreads and is harder to flip.
</Callout>

## Patience at the pan

The main cause of torn pancakes is an early flip. Wait for bubbles on the surface and set edges: that signals the underside is done and the spatula slides under easily. And keep the heat medium — on high, the top stays raw while the bottom scorches.
