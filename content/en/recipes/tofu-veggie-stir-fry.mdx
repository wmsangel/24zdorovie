Crispy tofu with vegetables is the case where a plant dish comes out filling and with decent protein, not "greens on the side". The secret is just two things: press the moisture out of the tofu and coat it in starch before frying. In 25 minutes you get a serving with 21 g of complete protein.

## The main secret is dry tofu

The downfall of most home attempts to fry tofu is water. Firm tofu is saturated with moisture, and if you throw it on the pan as is, it steams in its own juices and stays soft. So you press it under a weight, then toss it in starch: a thin crust seals on high heat and gives that crunch.

<Callout type="tip" title="If you have no time to press">
  Cut the tofu and blot each cube on all sides with paper towel, pressing lightly. It is worse than a full press under a weight, but noticeably better than nothing.
</Callout>

## About the protein

Soy is one of the few plants with a complete amino acid profile, so tofu counts as a complete protein source rather than an "incomplete" plant protein. 21 g per serving is a good result for a meatless dish, and served with rice or buckwheat it makes a balanced meal.
