Greek chicken salad is the case where a simple plate of vegetables becomes a proper lunch: 38 g of protein for 420 kcal, ready in 25 minutes. There are only two technical points that matter — don't overcook the breast, and don't dress the salad in advance. Everything else is chopping.

## Why it works as a meal

An ordinary vegetable salad leaves you hungry within the hour: it has little protein and few calories that sustain satiety. Two things change that here.

First, **protein**. The review by Leidy and colleagues found that protein delivers the greatest satiety of any macronutrient and helps preserve muscle mass during weight loss. Three hundred grams of chicken breast across two servings gives about 33 g of protein, with another 5 g from the feta.

Second, **volume**. Half a kilo of vegetables across two plates stretches the stomach and triggers mechanoreceptor satiety signals while adding only about 100 kcal. It is exactly that combination — high volume plus high protein — that makes the dish filling at a moderate calorie count.

The fat profile is favourable too: olive oil supplies monounsaturated fat, the foundation of the Mediterranean pattern that reduced cardiovascular events by roughly a third in high-risk people in the PREDIMED trial.

## Ingredients for two servings

- **Chicken breast — 300 g.** The main protein source. Thigh works too: juicier, but adds about 60 kcal per serving
- **Cucumbers — 200 g and tomatoes — 250 g.** The salad's base. Choose meaty tomatoes; watery ones flood the bowl
- **Bell pepper — 100 g.** Crunch and vitamin C, which also improves iron absorption
- **Red onion — 50 g.** Soak it in cold water, or it overwhelms everything else
- **Olives — 40 g.** Salt and character. Rinse them if they come in a strong brine
- **Feta — 100 g.** Contributes salt, fat and 5 g of protein per serving. With feta in the bowl, extra salt is rarely needed
- **Olive oil — 25 g and lemon juice — 20 g.** The classic roughly 1:1 ratio makes a light dressing
- **Oregano — 1 tsp.** Half in the marinade, half in the dressing. Dried suits this better than fresh

## Method: 25 minutes step by step

1. **Prep the chicken (3 minutes).** Butterfly the fillets or pound them to 1.5–2 cm. This is the crucial step: a thick breast dries on the outside before the centre is cooked.
2. **Marinade (10 minutes, hands-off).** Mix a tablespoon of oil, half the lemon juice, grated garlic, half the oregano, salt and pepper. Marinate the chicken and turn to the vegetables.
3. **Chopping (7 minutes).** Cucumbers into thick half-moons, tomatoes into wedges, pepper into strips, onion into thin slivers. The chunky cut isn't sloppiness but tradition: Greek salad is cut in pieces, not diced.
4. **Onion in water (5 minutes).** Cover the sliced onion with cold water, then pat dry. The harshness and bitterness go, the crunch stays.
5. **Cooking (10 minutes).** Hot pan, medium heat, 4–5 minutes a side. Don't flip repeatedly — you want a crust. Done at 74 °C internally.
6. **Rest (5 minutes).** Move to a board and don't cut immediately. Slicing hot meat sends the juices onto the board and leaves the breast dry.
7. **Assembly (2 minutes).** Whisk the dressing, combine vegetables and olives, dress and toss. Top with sliced chicken and feta crumbled by hand.

<Callout type="tip" title="On temperature and resting">
  Chicken breast loses moisture fast: at 74 °C it is done, at 80 °C it is noticeably drier. A thermometer settles this more accurately than any timer, because fillet thickness always varies. The five-minute rest isn't a formality: it lets the juices pushed towards the centre by heat redistribute through the fibres.
</Callout>

How to tailor it:

- **Fewer calories (about 340 kcal).** One tablespoon of oil instead of one and a half, and 60 g of feta instead of 100.
- **More substantial as a full meal.** Add 150 g of cooked bulgur or a slice of wholemeal bread — about 180 kcal and 5 g of protein more.
- **Vegetarian version.** Drop the chicken and add 300 g of chickpeas across two servings for around 21 g of protein per portion.
- **More fibre.** Add a handful of rocket or spinach and half an avocado — another 3–4 g of fibre.

## The bottom line

Greek chicken salad delivers 38 g of protein for 420 kcal in 25 minutes — a rare combination of high satiety, simplicity and a good fat profile. Two details decide it: even out the thickness of the breast and rest it after cooking, and dress the salad only at serving. The components keep separately for up to three days, which makes this an excellent option for prepping the week's lunches.
