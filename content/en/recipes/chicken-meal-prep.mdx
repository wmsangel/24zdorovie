One sheet pan and one hour on Sunday gets you four lunches at 53 g of protein each. The base is deliberately plain — roasted chicken breast, brown rice, broccoli and peppers — and the variety comes from a dressing you add on the day you eat it. Here is the full method, four sauces, and the part that actually matters in meal prep: the storage rules that keep Sunday's cooking safe to eat on Friday.

## Why breast, and why keep the base plain

Chicken breast is the densest practical source of protein on a normal grocery run: about 23 g of protein and under 2 g of fat per 100 g raw. That matters double for meal prep, because each container has to cover a real share of your daily protein. Otherwise you end up topping up at dinner anyway and the whole exercise saves nothing.

The second reason is predictability. Breast behaves the same on day four as on day one: it does not flake apart like fish or turn to paste when reheated the way ground meat does.

The real idea in this recipe, though, is the plain base. Salt, paprika, garlic, lemon — that is the whole seasoning. The standard meal prep mistake is marinating the entire batch in curry or teriyaki on Sunday, and by Wednesday that single flavor is hard to face. A neutral base plus a fresh dressing each day solves monotony completely.

<Callout type="tip" title="Even thickness beats every other trick">
  A chicken breast is wedge-shaped: the thin end is done at 12 minutes, the thick end at 25 — by which point the thin end is leather. Butterfly the thick end and open it out so the piece is an even 3/4 inch. This one step is the difference between juicy prep and sawdust.
</Callout>

## Ingredients for four servings

- **Chicken breasts, 1 3/4 lb (800 g).** About 7 oz raw per serving, which cooks down to roughly 5 oz of meat
- **Brown rice, 1 cup (200 g) dry.** 1/4 cup dry per portion, about 38 g of carbs. Swap in quinoa, bulgur or potatoes one-for-one by dry weight
- **Broccoli, 4 cups (400 g).** Holds its texture over four days better than any other vegetable
- **Bell peppers, 2 large (300 g).** The only sweetness in the base, plus a full day's vitamin C
- **Olive oil, 3 tbsp (40 g).** Half in the marinade, half on the vegetables
- **Garlic, smoked paprika, salt, pepper, lemon.** The entire flavor kit for the base

## The method, step by step

1. **Start the rice (5 min).** Oven to 400°F, rice into 2 cups of salted water, covered, 30 minutes. It now cooks itself while you do everything else.
2. **Prep the chicken (10 min).** Pat it dry with paper towels — wet meat steams instead of browning. Trim the silverskin and butterfly the thick end.
3. **Marinade (5 min).** Whisk half the oil with the crushed garlic, salt, smoked paprika, pepper, lemon zest and the juice of half the lemon. Coat the breasts and leave them 10 minutes at room temperature. Longer does nothing useful here.
4. **Vegetables (5 min).** Break the broccoli into florets, slice the peppers. Spread them on the sheet pan, toss with the remaining oil and salt using your hands, then push them to the edges.
5. **Roast (25 min).** Chicken in the center of the pan. Start probing at 22 minutes and pull it the moment the thickest part hits 165°F, even if that feels early.
6. **Rest and slice (5 min).** Foil-tent for 5 minutes so the juices redistribute, then slice across the grain into 1/2-inch pieces.
7. **Portion and cool.** Divide between four shallow containers, leave them open to cool, then lid and chill.

<Callout type="warning" title="Food safety is the core of meal prep">
  Chicken breast is done at **165°F** internal, measured with a thermometer in the thickest part. Cool it and get it into the refrigerator **within two hours** of leaving the oven — portioning into shallow uncovered containers is what makes that possible. Cooked chicken keeps **three to four days** at 40°F or below, so send Thursday's and Friday's portions to the freezer on cooking day, not on Thursday. Reheat all the way through to **165°F**, not merely to "warm." And never reheat the same portion twice: every extra cycle is more time in the 40–140°F danger zone where bacteria multiply fastest.
</Callout>

## Four dressings so it never gets old

Add the dressing right before eating, not when you portion. A sauce that has sat on the meat for four days softens it and flattens the flavor. Keep the sauces in separate small jars.

The logic: Monday and Thursday are creamy and acidic, Tuesday is herbal, Wednesday is salty and sharp. There is enough contrast between them that your brain registers four separate meals even though the chicken is identical.

The other lever is texture: stir fresh spinach into the container after reheating one day, add quick-pickled onion the next. A cold, crunchy element next to the hot base does more for perceived variety than a fifth sauce would.

## The storage protocol in full

**Cool it fast.** Do not leave the sheet pan on the stove "until later." Portion immediately and cool uncovered: a shallow layer chills in 30–40 minutes, a deep container takes hours spent in the danger zone.

**Refrigerate within two hours.** The clock starts when the food leaves the oven. Above 90°F ambient, that window shrinks to one hour.

**Three to four days.** That is the USDA FSIS window for cooked leftovers. Cooked Sunday evening means finished by Thursday.

**Freeze the back half of the week.** Two portions go straight into the freezer, where chicken and rice keep well for two to three months. Thaw them overnight in the refrigerator — never on the counter.

**Reheat to 165°F.** In a microwave, stir halfway through the cycle or the center stays cold. And the rule people break most often: do not reheat a portion a second time.

<Callout type="danger" title="When to throw it out, no debate">
  A slick or tacky surface on the meat, a sour or ammonia smell, cloudy liquid pooled in the bottom of the container, or any portion that spent the night out of the fridge — bin it. Tasting a small piece to check proves nothing: the toxin levels that make people sick do not change how food tastes. A portion of chicken costs far less than a night of food poisoning.
</Callout>

## Common mistakes

**Cooking to the clock instead of the thermometer.** Breasts vary in thickness by a factor of two and home ovens run 20–30°F off their dial. An instant-read thermometer pays for itself on the first batch.

**Lidding food while it is still hot.** Steam condenses, drips back down, and by Wednesday the broccoli is slimy. Close the lid only after the food has cooled.

**Slicing with the grain.** The pieces come out chewy and shred into strings when reheated. Always cut across.

**Dressing everything at portioning time.** By day three the sauce has soaked all the way through and every container tastes the same — precisely the problem this recipe is built to avoid.
