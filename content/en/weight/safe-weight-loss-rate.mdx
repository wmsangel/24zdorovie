A safe weight-loss rate for most people is about 0.5–1% of body weight a week — roughly 0.25–1 kg. Someone with a lot of excess weight can lose faster at first; a lean person should go slower. This is not "patience for its own sake": on a fast deficit you lose more muscle along with fat, face higher deficiency risk and a stronger rebound. Here is why slower is almost always more durable, and how to lose fat rather than muscle.

## Where the benchmark comes from

Rate is easier to think of as a percentage of body weight than in absolute kilograms: 1% for a 120 kg person and a 60 kg person are very different absolute figures and a different strain on the body. Hence the rule: **0.5–1% of body weight a week**.

The logic is simple. A calorie deficit makes the body draw energy from reserves. If the deficit is moderate, the main source is fat. If the deficit is too large, the body starts sacrificing more muscle too, while switching on defensive mechanisms: more hunger, lower expenditure.

## Why faster is not better

The appeal of a crash diet is obvious: the scale drops fast. But most of that fast "result" is water and glycogen, not fat, and it comes back easily. Worse, aggressive weight loss has a real cost:

- **Muscle loss.** The sharper the deficit and the less protein, the higher the share of muscle lost.
- **Deficiencies.** On very few calories it is hard to get enough vitamins and minerals.
- **Gallstones.** Rapid weight loss raises their risk.
- **Rebound.** Strong hunger and reduced expenditure push toward a relapse and weight regain.

<Callout type="science" title="Fast ≠ reliable">
  In studies, fast and slow rates can lead to similar weight loss over the short term, but the quality differs: a slower rate loses more fat and less muscle. And maintenance depends not on speed but on whether new habits became permanent.
</Callout>

## How to lose fat, not muscle

Three levers work together:

- **A moderate deficit** instead of starvation — keeps hunger and metabolism in check.
- **Enough protein** — about 1.6–2.2 g per kg of body weight: a signal to keep muscle.
- **Resistance training** — gives muscle a "reason" to stay.

## About plateaus and the scale

Weight almost never falls in a straight line: it jumps with water, gut contents, salt, and in women the cycle phase. Plus as you lose weight, daily expenditure falls and the old deficit shrinks. So it is better to track a 1–2 week average than a particular morning. A slowdown is a signal to review calories or add movement, not to starve harder.

The bottom line is simple: the goal is not "lose weight faster than anyone" but "lose it so it stays off". A slow, steady rate with protein and resistance training beats any express diet almost every time.
