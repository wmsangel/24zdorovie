    a: "Usually none — it stays silent for years and is found through blood tests, not by how you feel. Indirect clues include a growing waistline and visceral fat, high triglycerides with low HDL, darkened skin folds (acanthosis nigricans), and sluggishness or sugar cravings after carb-heavy meals.   - q: "How do you check for insulin resistance?"
    a: "Start with fasting glucose and HbA1c, which flag prediabetes and diabetes. Insulin sensitivity is estimated more directly by HOMA-IR (fasting glucose × fasting insulin / 22.5). The triglyceride-to-HDL ratio and waist circumference help indirectly. Interpret the numbers with a doctor."

Insulin resistance is when your cells respond less to insulin, so the pancreas has to make more of it to keep blood sugar normal. It isn't a diagnosis and it isn't diabetes — it's a precursor state: it runs quietly for years, is tied to visceral fat and cardiovascular risk, yet in its early stages it's **largely reversible** — without drugs, through boring but effective habits.

## What it is and why it matters

Insulin is the hormone that lets cells take in glucose. With insulin resistance, cells respond to it more weakly, the pancreas compensates by making more, and blood insulin rises **before** blood sugar does. That's why fasting glucose can still look normal while the process is already underway.

Over time the compensation stops being enough — that's how prediabetes develops, and then type 2 diabetes. In parallel, insulin resistance is linked to visceral fat, raised triglycerides and blood pressure — that is, to cardiovascular risk.

## Signs: why you can't rely on them

The main trap is that insulin resistance is **usually symptomless**. Indirect clues exist, but they're non-specific:

<KeyPoints title="Indirect clues (not a diagnosis)" items={[
  "A growing waist, abdominal fat",
  "High triglycerides and low HDL on a lipid panel",
  "Darkening and thickening of skin folds (acanthosis nigricans)",
  "Sluggishness and sugar cravings after carb-heavy meals",
  "In women — an association with PCOS"
]} />

None of these confirm or rule out the condition — only a blood test does.

The key idea: it's not about "cutting one food," but about reducing visceral fat and moving regularly. Muscle is the body's main glucose consumer, and it keeps taking up glucose even when insulin sensitivity is reduced.

## When medication and a doctor are needed

Insulin resistance isn't a standalone diagnosis treated with "a pill." But with prediabetes, diabetes, PCOS or high risk, a doctor may add medication (often metformin) on top of lifestyle. That's a specialist's decision based on your labs — not on a calculator or an article.

<Callout type="warning" title="This is not a diagnosis or self-treatment">
  Threshold numbers and HOMA-IR are a guide, not a verdict. A diagnosis is made by a doctor from the full set of labs and your history. Tools and this article help you understand your situation and ask the right questions, but they don't replace a check-up.
</Callout>

Insulin resistance sits in the same tangle as visceral fat, blood pressure and lipids — so it makes sense to look at it alongside your overall cardiovascular risk.
