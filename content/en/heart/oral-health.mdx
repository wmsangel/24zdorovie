Oral care seems simple, but the benefit is lost in the details — and this is where a lot of marketing lives. In short: **the base is fluoride toothpaste twice a day and daily cleaning between the teeth; an electric brush is slightly better than manual; and gum inflammation is linked not only to the mouth but to heart risk.**

## The base that works

It all rests on two things. First — **brushing with fluoride toothpaste twice a day**: fluoride strengthens enamel and reduces decay, one of the most reliably proven measures in dentistry. After the evening brush, spit but do not rinse with water — that keeps the fluoride working longer.

Second — **daily cleaning between the teeth**, where a brush cannot reach. Floss, interdental brushes or a water flosser — whatever is convenient and you will actually use. It is not optional: decay and inflammation often start between the teeth.

**An electric brush**, per the Cochrane review, removes plaque and reduces gum inflammation a little better than manual — especially oscillating-rotating heads. The difference is small, so technique and consistency matter more than the gadget.

**A water flosser** is a handy addition, especially where floss is awkward (braces, implants, bridges). No need to replace brushing with it — it works alongside the brush.

## Gums and the heart

Here a seemingly cosmetic matter reaches beyond the mouth. **Periodontitis** — chronic inflammation of the tissues around the teeth — is associated with a higher risk of cardiovascular disease. A consensus of European periodontologists and the World Heart Federation recognizes the link.

An important caveat: it is an **association, not a strictly proven cause**. Part of it is explained by shared factors — smoking and diabetes. But the logic is simple: chronic inflammation and bacteria from diseased gums enter the bloodstream, and chronic inflammation is a known factor in atherosclerosis. Healthy gums are not only about your smile.

## What harms

The main enemies of enamel are **frequent sugary snacks and acidic drinks** (soda, juice, energy drinks). It is not only the amount of sugar but the frequency: constant "acid attacks" through the day are worse than one dessert at once. And smoking is the leading modifiable factor in gum disease.

## Bottom line

Oral health is a dull but effective base: fluoride toothpaste, cleaning between teeth, a regular dentist. Gadgets add a small gain, not a miracle. And remember that healthy gums are also a contribution to a healthy heart.
