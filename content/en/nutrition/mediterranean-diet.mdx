The Mediterranean diet is not a set of exotic ingredients or another weight-loss challenge — it is an eating pattern reconstructed from how people ate in Crete, southern Italy and Greece in the mid-twentieth century. It regularly tops diet rankings, and not because of fashion: it carries the largest body of evidence of any dietary pattern, including randomised trials with hard endpoints — heart attacks, strokes and death, not just improved lab values.

## What the diet contains

The Mediterranean pattern is described by frequency rather than prohibition. Foods fall into daily, weekly and rare categories.

Note what is absent from that list: calorie counting, an allowed/forbidden split, elimination of whole macronutrients. Fat intake is fairly high — up to 35–40 % of calories — but predominantly monounsaturated fat from olive oil and polyunsaturated fat from nuts and fish.

## What the research shows

**PREDIMED** was a Spanish randomised trial of more than 7400 people at high cardiovascular risk but free of disease at baseline. Participants were assigned to a Mediterranean diet supplemented with extra-virgin olive oil, a Mediterranean diet supplemented with nuts, or a control group advised to reduce fat. After five years, the combined rate of heart attack, stroke and cardiovascular death was around 30 % lower in both Mediterranean arms. The trial was republished in 2018 after randomisation issues at some sites were re-analysed — the conclusions held.

The umbrella review by Dinu and colleagues, pooling dozens of meta-analyses, found consistent associations between adherence to this pattern and lower all-cause mortality, cardiovascular events, type 2 diabetes and several cancers. A separate line of evidence in cognitive aging links higher adherence to slower decline in memory and attention.

<Callout type="science" title="Why no single ingredient explains it">
  Attempts to reduce the effect to one food — olive oil, nuts or fish — keep failing. What works is synergy: replacing saturated fat with unsaturated, plenty of fibre and polyphenols, less sodium and ultra-processed food, regular legumes. A polyphenol capsule does not reproduce that.
</Callout>

## A six-week framework

You don't need to change everything at once. A stepwise transition works: one swap a week sticks far better than a total overhaul on a single Monday.

1. **Week 1.** Make olive or rapeseed oil your default fat instead of butter and margarine.
2. **Week 2.** Add legumes three times a week — soup, salad, stew.
3. **Week 3.** Two fish meals a week, at least one of them oily fish.
4. **Week 4.** Replace at least half your white bread and white rice with whole-grain versions.
5. **Week 5.** A handful of nuts instead of a snack, fruit instead of dessert.
6. **Week 6.** Cut red and processed meat to once or twice a week.

## Common mistakes

- **Pouring olive oil over everything.** Oil is energy-dense — 900 kcal per 100 g. PREDIMED used roughly 50 ml a day within the whole diet, not added on top of previous eating.
- **Pasta and white bread as the base.** In the original pattern grains are mostly whole and portions moderate. Tourist-menu Italian is not the diet of a 1960s Cretan.
- **Skipping legumes.** This is the most commonly dropped and most important category: fibre, plant protein, potassium.
- **Treating wine as part of the protocol.** Current WHO positions are unambiguous: no safe level of alcohol has been established, and no one should start drinking for their heart.
- **Buying superfoods instead of plain vegetables.** The benefit comes from the volume of plant food, not rare berries.

## The bottom line

The Mediterranean diet means a plant-forward base, olive oil as the main fat, legumes and whole grains every week, fish twice a week, and red meat and sweets only occasionally. Its strength lies in the combination, not in exotic ingredients: it is the best-evidenced eating pattern available, with cardiovascular events cut by about a third in high-risk people. It transfers well to local foods and needs no calorie counting — change one habit a week and in six weeks your diet looks different.
