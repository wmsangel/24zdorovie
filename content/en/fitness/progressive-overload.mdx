Progressive overload is the gradual increase of demand on your muscles from workout to workout. The principle is simple: the body adapts to exactly the load it is used to, so to keep growing in strength and size the stimulus must be raised little by little. Without this, even a perfect program eventually stops working. Here is how to add load correctly and what to do when progress stalls.

## Why you cannot do without it

Muscles and the nervous system adapt to load: what was heavy a month ago becomes routine. If the load does not rise, the body has no reason to change — and progress plateaus. Progressive overload is the way to give the body a little more than it can already do, time after time, keeping the stimulus for growth.

Important: "more" does not mean "to failure every time". It is controlled, gradual increase, not heroics every session.

## How to apply it in practice

A convenient, reliable method is **double progression**. Choose a rep range (say 8–12). Work with a weight until you reach the top of the range across all sets — then raise the weight a little and start again from the bottom of the range. This makes progress smooth and free of sharp jumps.

<Callout type="science" title="Linear only at first">
  In a beginner, strength rises fast, and weight can be added almost every session. This is temporary. With experience, progress slows and is counted in weeks and months. Trying to keep "plus a kilo every time" forever is a direct route to a stall and injury.
</Callout>

## When progress stalls

A plateau is not a failure but a signal. The order of actions:

1. Check recovery: sleep, protein, calories, overall stress.
2. Change a variable: if you have added weight for a long time, switch to reps or volume.
3. Take a deload week — lighter in weight and volume, to recover.
