If your first steps in the morning shoot a sharp pain through your heel, it's most often **plantar fasciitis**, the commonest cause of heel pain. The good news: it almost always resolves without surgery, and the proven base is simple — **stretching, load management and comfortable footwear**. Here's what works, what doesn't, and when to see a doctor after all.

## Why it hurts

The plantar fascia is a thick band along the sole from the heel to the base of the toes. When it's overloaded (a rapid jump in activity, long hours on your feet, stiff shoes, weight gain), the point where it attaches to the heel gets irritated. Hence the characteristic pattern: sharp pain on the first steps in the morning or after sitting, easing a little as you move but returning by evening.

## About footwear

During a flare, what matters isn't stylish shoes but comfortable ones: cushioned, with arch support and not dead-flat. At home, instead of walking barefoot on tile, wearing shoes or slippers with a thick soft sole takes some of the impact off the heel. For active days, choose well-cushioned trainers; for recovery and around-the-house or post-workout wear, soft cushioning footwear helps.
