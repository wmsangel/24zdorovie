Short answer: **for recreational mileage, running is more good than bad for your knees**. The idea that running "grinds down" your joints didn't survive scrutiny — recreational runners get arthritis less often than people who don't move. The trouble comes not from the runs themselves but from ramping load up too fast. Here's what the research shows and what to do if your knee does hurt.

## What the research shows

A large 2017 meta-analysis pooled data on runners and sedentary people. Knee and hip osteoarthritis appeared in **about 3.5% of recreational runners** versus **~10% of sedentary people**. Elite runners with very high mileage, by contrast, had higher risk — so the relationship is U-shaped: both doing nothing and doing extreme volumes are harmful, and moderate running lands in the protective middle.

Mechanically this makes sense: cartilage doesn't wear like a brake pad. It's living tissue nourished by joint movement that adapts to cyclic load. Modelling of running loads doesn't support the idea that ordinary running triggers arthritis.

What helps: temporarily reduce volume (don't stop entirely), strengthen hips, glutes and calves, and return gradually. Build sensibly — a rough guide is no more than ~10% more volume a week.
