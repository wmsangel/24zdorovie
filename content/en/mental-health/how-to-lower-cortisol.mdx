A healthy person doesn't need to lower cortisol as a goal — it isn't a toxin but a vital hormone that governs waking up, metabolism and your response to stress. The problem isn't cortisol itself but the chronic stress and sleep loss that disrupt its daily rhythm. So what works isn't a «cortisol detox» but the boring basics: sleep, movement, less caffeine and alcohol, managing your load. Genuinely high cortisol as a disease is rare and needs a doctor, not a supplement.

## What cortisol is and why you need it

Cortisol is made by your adrenal glands on a signal from the brain (the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis). It isn't a «villain hormone» but a conductor of your daily rhythm: highest in the morning to help you wake and switch on, tapering by night to make room for melatonin.

Beyond the wake-up call, cortisol does plenty that's useful:

<KeyPoints title="What cortisol does every day" items={[
  "Raises blood sugar, fuelling your muscles and brain",
  "Regulates blood pressure and vascular tone",
  "Dampens inflammation — the basis of glucocorticoid medicines",
  "Mobilises you under acute stress — the classic «fight or flight» response"
]} />

A complete absence of cortisol is incompatible with life. So the goal isn't to «zero it out» but to keep a normal daily rhythm: high in the morning, low at night.

## «Chronically high cortisol»: myth vs reality

Two things get constantly blurred online, and it's worth separating them.

**The real pathology is Cushing's syndrome.** This rare condition means cortisol is steadily and strongly elevated (from a tumour or long-term glucocorticoid use). It has recognisable signs: a rounded, flushed face, fat on the abdomen and neck while arms and legs thin, purple stretch marks, fragile bones, high blood pressure. It's a diagnosis an endocrinologist makes from tests — and it doesn't come from a hectic week at work.

**Everyday stress is different.** In most people with fatigue, anxiety and poor sleep, cortisol measures within the normal range. That's exactly why the popular «adrenal fatigue» label isn't recognised by medicine: a systematic review of 58 studies found neither evidence for the condition nor a reliable way to «measure» it.

<Callout type="warning" title="Why this matters">
  The distinction isn't academic. Blaming real fatigue on «exhausted adrenals» and treating them with supplements makes it easy to miss the actual cause — iron deficiency, an underactive thyroid, depression, sleep apnoea or plain chronic sleep loss. Fatigue deserves a workup, not a label.
</Callout>

## What actually raises cortisol

Cortisol is a rapid-response system, and normally it rises and falls on its own. But a few factors keep it elevated longer than it should be.

The key word is «reverse». A healthy stress system is supposed to produce cortisol spikes — that's a normal response to a deadline or a workout. What harms isn't the spike but the missing fall — when stress and sleep loss stop the level returning to baseline day after day.

## The myths that sell

A whole industry is built around cortisol. Here are the most common claims.

<Callout type="danger" title="What not to believe">
  **«Cortisol face» and «cortisol belly».** A puffy face and belly are sold as signs of stress to move a course or a supplement. A true moon face is a symptom of Cushing's syndrome, not a rough week. Ordinary morning puffiness is more often salt, alcohol and poor sleep.

  **«Cortisol detox».** Cortisol isn't a toxin and doesn't accumulate — there's nothing to «flush out», and no way to. The body regulates it itself.

  **Stacks of «anti-cortisol» supplements.** Most have never been tested in proper studies. The marketing exploits anxiety, not data.
</Callout>

## What genuinely keeps cortisol in check

The good news: it's exactly what's good for you regardless of cortisol.

**Sleep first.** Sleep loss is one of the most reliable ways to raise cortisol, and normal sleep restores the rhythm. It's the most underrated «anti-stress pill» there is.

**Move — but don't overdo it.** Moderate regular activity lowers your stress reactivity. Meanwhile ultra-long, punishing sessions without recovery raise cortisol — balance beats «more is better».

**Stress management with real evidence.** Slow breathing with a long exhale, mindfulness practices and cognitive-behavioural techniques genuinely calm the stress axis — unlike «detoxes».

**Less caffeine and alcohol.** Both hit your cortisol rhythm and your sleep. Moving your last coffee to the morning and cutting back on alcohol often does more than any supplement.

**As for supplements — maybe ashwagandha.** It's the one popular option with any data: several small RCTs found it reduced perceived stress and cortisol. The effect is modest and study quality is mixed, so treat it as an add-on to sleep and routine, not a replacement.

## When to see a doctor

Wanting to «lower cortisol» is not itself a reason for tests. But some symptom clusters do warrant an endocrinologist.

<Callout type="info" title="Discuss with a doctor if you have">
  Rapid weight gain redistributing to the belly and face while limbs thin; purple stretch marks wider than 1 cm; new high blood pressure at a young age; easy bruising and thinning skin; in women, marked male-pattern hair growth and cycle changes. Individually these occur without Cushing's, but together they're a reason to check cortisol.
</Callout>

Testing is ordered by a doctor and not from a single blood draw: cortisol varies too much through the day. They use late-night salivary cortisol, 24-hour urinary free cortisol, or a dexamethasone suppression test. Checking it «just in case» without symptoms only means worrying over natural fluctuations.

## The bottom line

Cortisol is nothing to fear and nothing to «lower» for its own sake. A healthy person is better served by fixing sleep, movement and stress — then the hormone's daily rhythm restores itself. «Detoxes», «anti-cortisol» supplements and labels like «cortisol face» are about marketing, not health. And the rare case of genuinely high cortisol is a disease a doctor finds through tests, not through your feed.
