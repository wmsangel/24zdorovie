Eyes attract a lot of scare stories: "screens will wreck your vision," "reading in the dark is harmful," "blue-light glasses will save you." By the evidence: **screens cause temporary fatigue, not disease; blue-light glasses are useless; and what really matters for your eyes is time outdoors in childhood, UV protection, and regular check-ups.**

## Screens: fatigue, not disease

Long screen work really does make your eyes feel worse — it is called digital eye strain. The mechanism is simple: at a screen we **blink far less often**, the tear film evaporates, and dryness, irritation and blurring appear. The muscles that focus up close also tire.

The key word is **temporary**. It clears after a break, and there is no solid evidence that screens cause eye disease in adults. What helps is not "special glasses" but simple things: breaks, conscious blinking, lubricating drops, a normal distance and no glare.

<Callout type="warning" title="Blue-light glasses don't work">
  The 2023 Cochrane review found no benefit from blue-light-filtering lenses: they do not reduce screen-related eye strain and do not improve sleep. It is one of the best-sold but unproven options in optics.
</Callout>

## Myopia: why time outdoors matters

Myopia is rising worldwide, and here the screen is not the main villain. The evidence points to two factors: **a lot of near work and too little daylight**. In a randomized trial in China, extra outdoor time at school markedly reduced new cases of myopia.

The practical takeaway for parents: **about 2 hours a day outdoors** is the best-proven prevention. If myopia is already progressing, an ophthalmologist can add specific methods (low-dose atropine, special lenses). In adults myopia is usually stable and cannot be walked back.

On **sunglasses**: they are not just comfort. Years of UV exposure are linked to cataracts, so the UV400 marking matters more than how dark the lens is — dark lenses without a UV filter are actually worse, because the pupil widens and lets in more ultraviolet.

## When to get your eyes checked

A regular exam catches not only "I can't see as well" but diseases that do not hurt or interfere early on — glaucoma, retinal changes. A guide: **every 1–2 years** for adults without complaints, more often after 40 (presbyopia), with diabetes, high myopia, or glaucoma in the family.

## Bottom line

Eyes are not "ruined" by screens — they get tired, and that reverses. There is no point spending on blue-light glasses. The dull things are what work: time outdoors for kids, UV protection, breaks, and regular eye exams after 40.
