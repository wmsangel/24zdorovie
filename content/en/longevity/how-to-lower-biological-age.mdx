You can't reverse the calendar, but "biological age" isn't the calendar — it's a number calculated from biomarkers like blood glucose, inflammation, liver and kidney markers, fitness and body composition. Those genuinely respond to how you live. So lowering your biological age is real in a specific sense: you improve the inputs the score is built from. And the levers that move them most are unglamorous and mostly free — not the supplements sold alongside the idea.

## What "lowering biological age" really means

Biological-age scores come in two families. Epigenetic clocks read DNA methylation; composite scores like **PhenoAge** combine nine routine blood markers with your age. Both are estimates of how your body is faring relative to the calendar — and both are just functions of measurable inputs.

That's the useful framing. You're not chasing a mystical rejuvenation; you're moving glucose, blood pressure, inflammation (CRP), lipids, fitness and muscle in the right direction. Do that and the number follows. Chase the number directly — through a supplement that promises to "reset your age" — and you usually move nothing real.

If you do only one thing, make it **fitness**. In a study of over 120,000 people, higher cardiorespiratory fitness tracked with lower mortality across the board, with no observed ceiling of benefit — and unlike your chronological age, fitness is trainable at any age.

## What's oversold

The longevity market runs ahead of the evidence, and biological age is its favourite hook.

<Callout type="warning" title="Where the money goes, not the evidence">
  NMN, NAD+ boosters, resveratrol and most "longevity" stacks rest on animal and cell studies, not human outcomes. No supplement has been shown to lower biological age or extend healthy lifespan in people. Expensive epigenetic-clock subscriptions have their own problem: the same blood sample can return biological ages several years apart, so a single reading — or a small "improvement" after buying the product — tells you little.
</Callout>

This isn't nihilism. It's triage: the boring, free levers have decades of evidence, and the paid shortcuts don't. Spend your effort and money in that order.

## How to track it honestly

You don't need a subscription to see whether this is working. A standard blood panel already contains most of what PhenoAge uses — glucose, CRP, albumin, liver and kidney markers, white cell count — and repeating it once or twice a year shows the trend that matters.

Pair that with a few things a lab can't sell you: resting heart rate, waist circumference, how much you can lift, and how you handle a brisk 20 minutes. Those move first, and they move fastest.
