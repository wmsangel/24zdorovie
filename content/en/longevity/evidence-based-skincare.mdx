The skincare industry runs on complexity: ten steps, a serum for every letter of the alphabet, "glow" and "detox." But the list of what is actually backed by research is short. Strip out the marketing and you are left with **three pillars: sun protection, retinoids and moisturizer** — plus a couple of proven actives. A short routine built on these works better than any long ritual.

## Pillar 1. Sun protection

Ultraviolet light is the main external cause of skin aging: wrinkles, pigmentation and loss of firmness on exposed areas track back to it. And this is one of the few skincare points proven rigorously.

In an Australian randomized trial, daily sunscreen over 4.5 years noticeably slowed photoaging compared with discretionary use. Separately, sunscreen is proven skin-cancer prevention. You need **broad-spectrum (UVA + UVB), SPF 30 or higher**, applied generously and reapplied in the sun.

<Callout type="science" title="Why sunscreen comes first">
  Most "anti-aging" claims rest on lab surrogates and before-and-after ads. Sunscreen is the rare exception with a multi-year randomized trial on a real outcome (skin condition, cancer risk). If you pick one product, this is it.
</Callout>

## Pillar 2. Retinoids

Retinoids (vitamin A derivatives) are the best-evidenced class of anti-aging actives. They stimulate collagen production, speed cell turnover and, in controlled studies, reduce wrinkles and even out texture.

- **Tretinoin** — prescription, stronger and faster.
- **Retinol** — over-the-counter, gentler: the skin first converts it to the same active form, so results come more slowly.

Start with a low concentration **2–3 nights a week**, building up as tolerated. Dryness and flaking in the first weeks are expected. Sunscreen the next day is mandatory.

## Pillar 3. Moisturizer and barrier

A moisturizer does not "rejuvenate," but it keeps the skin barrier intact — and without a working barrier, retinoids and acids are poorly tolerated. The working components: **humectants** (glycerin, hyaluronic acid) pull in water, **emollients and ceramides** rebuild the lipid layer.

Hence a practical point: scrubbing "until it squeaks" harms the barrier. Gentle cleansing with a pH-neutral product is enough.

## What not to expect from skincare

- **Collagen creams** do not replace your own collagen: the molecule is too large for the outer layer. Production is stimulated by retinoids and sun protection.
- **"Detox" and "toxin removal"** through the skin is a marketing cliché with no physiological basis.
- **Pricier is not more effective.** Ingredients and consistency decide, not cost. Premium is justified only insofar as it makes you use the routine every day.

## The working minimum

**Morning:** gentle cleanse → moisturizer → sunscreen. **Evening:** cleanse → retinoid (not nightly at first) → moisturizer. Everything else is optional and by tolerance. This short routine covers almost all the proven benefit of skincare; long rituals add time and irritation risk, not results.
