Autophagy is a built-in "cleanup" mechanism inside the cell: it breaks down damaged components and recycles them into building material. In animals, fasting does boost this process, and an entire industry of promises about "cleaning your cells" and longevity has been built on it. The problem is that almost all the bold conclusions come from animals and cells, while in humans specific claims — like "autophagy switches on after 16 hours" — are not yet proven. Here is where the science ends and the extrapolation begins.

## What autophagy is

The word literally means "self-eating". It is the cell's way of keeping itself in order: it packages worn-out proteins and organelles, breaks them down, and puts the freed molecules back to use. The mechanism is so fundamental that decoding it won the Nobel Prize in 2016.

The key point: autophagy runs **continuously**, not "switched on by a button" when you are hungry. Fasting, exercise and some stressors can increase it, but this is adjusting a level, not an "on/off" switch.

<Callout type="science" title="Where the evidence ends">
  Autophagy is well studied in yeast, worms, mice and cells. In humans it is hard to measure in living tissue, so transferring precise numbers ("16 hours", "after a day") from animals to people is not valid. The mechanism is real; specific "for autophagy" protocols in humans are extrapolation.
</Callout>

## Fasting: what is actually shown in humans

Let us separate two different questions. First, whether intermittent fasting helps health in principle. Here there is evidence: for some people it makes weight loss easier and improves metabolic markers. But in studies where calories are matched, intermittent fasting usually does **not outperform** an ordinary deficit — meaning it works mainly because the person eats less.

Second, whether fasting extends life through autophagy. Here there is no solid human data. Calorie restriction extends lifespan in several animals, but transferring this to humans remains a hypothesis, and no one has shown a direct "autophagy → longevity" link in people.

## What this means in practice

If intermittent eating helps you eat less and hold your weight, it is a reasonable tool with possible metabolic upsides. But fasting specifically "for autophagy and life extension" has no basis yet: in humans, the benefit of this particular mechanism is not proven, and the promises often run ahead of the science.
