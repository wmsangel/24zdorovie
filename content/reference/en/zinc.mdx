## What it is

Zinc is an essential trace mineral involved in hundreds of enzyme reactions. It's needed for immune cells, wound healing, taste and smell, and protein and DNA synthesis. The body stores very little, so intake must be regular — but from food, not from a bottle.

## Who it may help

Vegetarians and vegans (plant zinc is less well absorbed), people with a genuinely poor diet, and some gut conditions with impaired absorption. A healthy person on a normal diet doesn't need a separate supplement.

## How much to take

- **Requirement:** 8 mg/day for women, 11 mg/day for men.
- **Upper limit:** 40 mg/day (including supplements). Best taken with food — on an empty stomach it can cause nausea.

<Callout type="science" title="About lozenges for colds">
  The effect is described specifically for dissolving lozenges started within 24 hours. Swallowed tablets haven't been studied this way, and the overall evidence quality is low — don't expect a miracle.
</Callout>

## Food sources

Oysters (by far the richest), red meat, poultry, legumes, nuts, seeds, dairy. In plant foods zinc is bound to phytates and absorbed less well — which is what puts vegetarians in the at-risk group.

## Cautions and myths

<Callout type="warning" title="The main danger is excess">
  Chronic intake above 40 mg/day displaces copper and eventually causes copper deficiency and weakened immunity. Intranasal zinc has been linked to loss of smell — don't use it.
</Callout>

- **"More zinc, stronger immunity."** The opposite: chronic excess weakens it.
- **"Zinc prevents colds."** No preventive effect.

## Proven vs hyped

- **Proven:** correcting deficiency, helping diarrhoea in deficient children.
- **Hyped:** cold prevention, chronic "immune" megadoses, intranasal forms.
