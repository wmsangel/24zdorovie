## What it is

Iron is a component of haemoglobin (oxygen transport in blood), myoglobin and several enzymes. The body holds roughly 2.5–4 g, most of it in haemoglobin. When stores run low, iron-deficiency anaemia develops — the most common nutritional deficiency worldwide (WHO).

It is not an “energy tonic”: without confirmed shortfall, a supplement will not fix fatigue and raises overload risk.

## How much you need and where it comes from

Daily targets (RDA, NIH ODS): 8 mg for men 19+ and women 51+, 18 mg for women 19–50, 27 mg in pregnancy. For vegetarians, NIH suggests a target about 1.8× higher — non-heme iron absorbs less well. The adult upper limit is 45 mg/day; higher amounts only under medical care for deficiency.

Heme iron (meat, poultry, fish) absorbs better than non-heme (legumes, tofu, fortified cereals, spinach). Vitamin C boosts non-heme absorption; tea and coffee (polyphenols) and calcium reduce it.

## Who may run low

Risk groups per NIH ODS: women with heavy periods, pregnant people, infants and young children, frequent blood donors, vegetarians and vegans, GI disease (coeliac, IBD) and post-bariatric status. In a healthy man with an ordinary diet, deficiency is uncommon.

## Forms and absorption

Ferrous iron (sulfate, gluconate, fumarate) absorbs better than ferric. Dose for deficiency is set by a clinician using ferritin; take with a vitamin C source, apart from tea, coffee and calcium supplements.

The takeaway: supplements work where labs confirm a shortfall. Without deficiency, “iron for fatigue” is empty and risky.

## How to take it

Only with confirmed deficiency, prescribed and followed with ferritin. With vitamin C, not washed down with tea or coffee, timed away from calcium. Not as a casual “course just in case,” and not without labs.

## Cautions and myths

<Callout type="warning" title="When to be careful">
  Do not take iron without confirmed deficiency: overload risk, especially with hemochromatosis. Supplements are a common cause of poisoning in children — keep them out of reach. Overdose brings nausea, constipation and abdominal pain. The adult upper limit is 45 mg/day.
</Callout>

- **“I’m tired, so I must be low on iron.”** Do not start without labs (ferritin): fatigue is usually not about iron, and taking iron without deficiency is useless and unsafe.
- **“More is better.”** Above 45 mg/day without medical care for deficiency adds overload risk without extra benefit.
