## What it is

Vitamin C is a water-soluble vitamin the body can neither make nor store. It's needed for collagen synthesis (so for skin, blood vessels and wound healing), acts as an antioxidant, aids iron absorption and supports immune cells. Severe deficiency is scurvy — a disease now rare in developed countries.

## Who it may help

A supplement is reasonable with a diet poor in fruit and vegetables, in smokers (higher needs), or under heavy physical load. Most healthy people don't need a separate supplement — the requirement is met from food.

## How much to take

- **Requirement:** 75 mg/day for women, 90 mg/day for men; +35 mg for smokers.
- **Upper limit:** 2000 mg/day. There's no point going above ~200 mg for a healthy person — the body saturates and the excess leaves in urine.

<Callout type="science" title="Why 'loading doses' don't work">
  Vitamin C absorption is capped: the larger the dose, the smaller the fraction absorbed, and the surplus is excreted by the kidneys. So "1000 mg for immunity" is mostly expensive urine.
</Callout>

## Food sources

Bell peppers, kiwi, citrus, berries, broccoli, sauerkraut. One pepper or kiwi covers the daily requirement. Prolonged cooking destroys some of it — choose fresh and lightly processed.

## Cautions and myths

<Callout type="warning" title="When not to overdo it">
  Doses above 2000 mg/day can cause diarrhoea and stomach pain, and in people prone to kidney stones can raise the risk of oxalate stones.
</Callout>

- **"Vitamin C protects against colds."** No preventive effect in ordinary people.
- **"More is better."** No: the excess is simply excreted.
- **"Effervescent tablets are better."** It's the same ascorbic acid, often with added sugar.

## Proven vs hyped

- **Proven:** preventing scurvy, aiding iron absorption, slightly shortening a cold with regular intake.
- **Hyped:** cold prevention, "immune" megadoses, an anti-cancer effect.
