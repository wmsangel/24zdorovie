## What it is

Omega-3 is a class of polyunsaturated fatty acids. Three matter in practice: EPA and DHA (from fish and algae) and ALA (from plants — flax, chia, walnuts). The body converts ALA to EPA and DHA only weakly, so for "marine" omega-3 levels it is fish and its products that do the work.

The main confusion is carrying "high doses lower triglycerides in people who have too many" over to "everyone, for the heart". In generally healthy people that has not held up.

## Who it's for

Mainly people who rarely eat fish: vegans and vegetarians (algal oil for them), and anyone with a fish allergy or dislike. Separately, pregnant women for DHA if fish intake is low (dose and choice are best discussed with a doctor). For everyone else, getting omega-3 from food is simpler and cheaper.

## How to choose

- Count the **EPA + DHA per serving**, not the total weight of "fish oil". A "1,000 mg" capsule may hold only ~300 mg.
- Form (triglyceride, ethyl ester) affects absorption moderately; the dose of EPA+DHA and freshness matter more (rancid fish oil is useless).
- Vegans — **algal oil**: the same EPA/DHA without the fish.

## Sources

Oily sea fish: salmon, mackerel, herring, sardines. Two servings a week, and most people need no supplement. Plant sources (flax, chia, walnuts) supply ALA — useful, but they barely raise EPA/DHA levels.

## Cautions and common myths

<Callout type="warning" title="When to be careful">
  High doses thin the blood slightly — discuss it with a doctor if you take anticoagulants or are heading into surgery. Don't exceed the FDA ceiling: up to 3 g/day of EPA+DHA combined, of which up to 2 g from supplements.
</Callout>

- **"Everyone needs omega-3 for the heart."** In generally healthy people, large trials have mostly shown no benefit.
- **"More fish oil is better."** What counts is the EPA+DHA dose, not the grams of oil on the label.
- **"Flax oil replaces fish."** It does not: ALA converts to EPA/DHA only weakly.

## Proven vs hyped

- **Proven:** lowering high triglycerides at treatment doses; a contribution to fetal development (DHA).
- **Hyped:** "for the heart, for everyone", joints, mood, and replacing fish with flax oil.
