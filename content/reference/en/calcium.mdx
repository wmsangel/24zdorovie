## What it is

Calcium is the body's most abundant mineral: 99% sits in bones and teeth, the rest works in muscle, nerves and blood clotting. Bone is constantly remodelled, and maintaining its density needs a steady calcium supply — especially with age, when losses rise.

## Who it may help

People who eat little dairy and other calcium sources; postmenopausal women; those with osteoporosis (on a doctor's advice). If your diet already meets the requirement, a supplement adds nothing.

## How much to take

- **Requirement:** 1000 mg/day; 1200 mg/day for women 50+ and men 70+.
- **Form and timing:** carbonate absorbs better with food, citrate any time; split single doses above ~500 mg for better absorption.
- **Upper limit:** 2000–2500 mg/day in total.

<Callout type="science" title="The core principle">
  First count calcium from food, then use a supplement only to fill the gap to the requirement. Taking calcium "just in case" on top of an adequate diet is pointless and potentially not harmless.
</Callout>

## Food sources

Dairy, fortified plant drinks, tofu (set with calcium sulphate), canned fish with bones (sardines), leafy greens (broccoli, kale), almonds.

## Cautions and myths

<Callout type="warning" title="When to be careful">
  Excess calcium from supplements can cause constipation and raise kidney-stone risk. People with kidney disease or on medication should clear the dose with their doctor.
</Callout>

- **"Everyone needs calcium pills for bones."** No: on an adequate diet a supplement doesn't help.
- **"More means stronger bones."** Beyond the requirement it adds no strength.

## Proven vs hyped

- **Proven:** supporting bone when intake is low, fewer fractures in deficient people (with vitamin D).
- **Hyped:** supplements on top of an adequate diet, "calcium for everyone".
